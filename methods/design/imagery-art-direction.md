# Imagery and Art Direction

## What
Imagery and Art Direction use screenshots, product visuals, photos, illustrations, and media to communicate real value.

Images should reveal, not merely decorate.

## Why it matters
Visual assets shape trust and comprehension quickly.

Strong imagery:
- makes the product concrete
- supports the message
- improves recall
- builds trust
- helps users inspect what matters

The image should answer a user question.

## When to apply
- landing pages
- product pages
- feature sections
- case studies
- onboarding
- empty states
- marketing pages

## How to apply

- show real product states when inspection matters
- match imagery to the surrounding claim
- avoid vague atmospheric visuals
- preserve important detail in crops
- optimise image performance
- use captions or labels when needed

Imagery should make the product easier to understand.

## Implementation rules

- product visuals must be legible
- images must support nearby copy
- decorative images must not distract from action
- important screenshots should not be overly blurred or cropped
- media needs responsive sizing and alt text
- image choice must match brand and audience

## Example

Bad:
- abstract hero image unrelated to product
- screenshot cropped so details cannot be inspected
- stock image replaces actual product proof

Good:
- clear product screenshot
- visual demonstrates the feature claim
- crop highlights the decision point
- alt text describes useful content

## Fail conditions

- image does not support the message
- product cannot be inspected
- visual feels generic or stock-like
- image slows page without value
- alt text is missing where meaning matters

## Enforcement rule

If imagery does not clarify product value or support trust, replace it with a more useful visual.
