# Captions and Transcripts

## What
Captions and Transcripts provide text alternatives for audio and video content.

Media must be understandable without hearing or watching in ideal conditions.

## Why it matters
Users may be deaf, hard of hearing, in noisy environments, or unable to play audio.

Good media alternatives:
- preserve meaning
- support comprehension
- improve search and scanning
- help users review content
- support accessibility compliance

Content should not depend on a single sense.

## When to apply
- product videos
- demos
- webinars
- audio clips
- testimonials
- tutorials
- animated explainers with narration

## How to apply

- provide captions for spoken audio
- include important non-speech sounds
- provide transcripts for long media
- sync captions accurately
- keep captions readable
- avoid putting essential content only in video

Users should be able to access the same information through text.

## Implementation rules

- prerecorded video with speech needs captions
- audio-only content needs a transcript
- captions must be accurate enough to preserve meaning
- important sound cues must be described
- captions must not cover critical visuals
- transcript links must be easy to find

## Example

Bad:
- silent demo with no text labels
- product video with speech but no captions
- transcript hidden behind unrelated navigation

Good:
- captioned product walkthrough
- transcript below webinar
- text summary beside demo
- sound cues included where useful

## Fail conditions

- spoken content has no text alternative
- captions are inaccurate
- transcript is missing for long audio
- important visual context is unexplained
- captions are unreadable

## Enforcement rule

If media communicates important information, provide an accurate text alternative.
