# Differentiation

## What
Differentiation makes the product meaningfully distinct from alternatives.

Different must be useful, not just novel.

## Why it matters
Users need a reason to choose one product over another.

Strong differentiation:
- improves memorability
- supports positioning
- reduces price-only comparison
- strengthens value perception
- increases confidence in the choice

If users cannot see the difference, they default to the familiar option.

## When to apply
- homepages
- comparison pages
- pricing pages
- product launches
- feature pages
- sales pages
- category pages

## How to apply

- identify the main alternative
- explain what is meaningfully different
- connect difference to user value
- show evidence of the difference
- avoid generic superiority claims
- make the tradeoff honest if relevant

Differentiation must help the user decide.

## Implementation rules

- difference must be specific
- difference must matter to the target user
- claims must be supported by proof, examples, or product behaviour
- avoid "better" without explaining why
- avoid feature dumping as differentiation
- do not create false contrast

## Example

Bad:
- "The best platform"
- "10x better"
- long feature list with no point of view
- vague comparison against everyone

Good:
- clear alternative
- specific difference
- outcome tied to that difference
- proof or example showing it

## Fail conditions

- user cannot explain why this product is different
- difference is irrelevant
- differentiation is unsupported
- comparison is vague or unfair
- product sounds like every competitor

## Enforcement rule

If the difference does not change the user's decision, make the distinction clearer or remove it.
