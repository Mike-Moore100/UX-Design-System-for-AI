# Competitive Alternatives

## What
Competitive Alternatives identifies what users would use or do if the product did not exist.

Real alternatives include competitors, manual workarounds, internal tools, and doing nothing.

## Why it matters
Users compare options before they commit.

Understanding alternatives:
- sharpens positioning
- improves objection handling
- clarifies differentiation
- strengthens sales pages
- reveals switching costs

Ignoring alternatives makes messaging naive.

## When to apply
- positioning
- comparison pages
- sales pages
- pricing
- enterprise messaging
- onboarding
- feature prioritisation

## How to apply

- list direct competitors
- include indirect substitutes and workarounds
- identify why users choose each alternative
- define where the product wins and loses
- address switching costs honestly
- make differentiation concrete

The user should understand why this option is better for their situation.

## Implementation rules

- alternatives must reflect real user behaviour
- comparison must be truthful
- differentiation must be specific
- weaknesses must not be hidden where they affect fit
- switching costs must be acknowledged
- avoid vague competitor bashing

## Example

Bad:
- says "unlike everyone else" without details
- ignores spreadsheets or manual processes
- creates unfair comparison table
- hides where the product is not a fit

Good:
- clear alternatives
- specific tradeoffs
- honest fit guidance
- differentiation tied to user problem

## Fail conditions

- real alternatives are missing
- comparison is biased or vague
- user cannot tell when to choose the product
- switching effort is ignored
- claims could apply to any competitor

## Enforcement rule

If users have meaningful alternatives, name the tradeoffs honestly and explain where the product is the better fit.
