# Proof Stack

## What
Proof Stack combines multiple forms of evidence to make a product claim credible.

Proof should support the specific claim being made, not decorate the page.

## Why it matters
Users discount claims without evidence.

A strong proof stack:
- increases trust
- reduces perceived risk
- supports differentiation
- answers objections
- improves sales confidence

One testimonial rarely proves a broad promise.

## When to apply
- landing pages
- pricing pages
- enterprise pages
- launch pages
- case studies
- product pages
- sales materials

## How to apply

- identify the main claims that need proof
- match proof type to claim type
- combine social, quantitative, qualitative, product, and authority proof
- place proof near the claim or decision point
- use specific details over generic praise
- keep proof current and verifiable

The user should see evidence before they are asked to believe.

## Implementation rules

- every major claim must have relevant support
- testimonials must include enough context to be credible
- metrics must define what was measured
- logos must be truthful and permissioned where needed
- case studies must show before, action, and result
- avoid proof that is impressive but irrelevant

## Example

Bad:
- logo wall with no context
- "Loved by teams" with no evidence
- metric without source or definition
- testimonial unrelated to the page claim

Good:
- claim-specific testimonial
- clear metric and context
- customer example
- product screenshot proving capability

## Fail conditions

- claim is unsupported
- proof is vague
- proof appears too far from the claim
- evidence is outdated
- proof creates legal or trust risk

## Enforcement rule

If a claim would affect purchase intent, attach relevant proof before asking the user to act.
