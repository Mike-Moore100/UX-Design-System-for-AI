# Launch Sequencing

## What
Launch Sequencing orders announcements, proof, education, and conversion moments so the market can understand and act on a product release.

Launches need momentum, not one isolated announcement.

## Why it matters
Users need repeated context before they care.

Strong launch sequencing:
- builds anticipation
- clarifies the story
- educates the audience
- creates proof early
- converts attention into action

A launch without sequence often spikes and disappears.

## When to apply
- product launches
- feature releases
- waitlists
- rebrands
- new categories
- campaigns
- major integrations

## How to apply

- define the launch audience and desired action
- create pre-launch education for the problem
- reveal the product promise clearly
- support the announcement with proof or demos
- give users a specific next step
- follow up with use cases, objections, and customer stories

The launch should move people from awareness to understanding to action.

## Implementation rules

- launch message must have one primary promise
- sequence must include before, launch, and follow-up moments
- each asset must have a clear job
- proof must be planned, not added late
- CTA must match user readiness at each stage
- success metrics must include activation or qualified intent

## Example

Bad:
- single launch post with feature list
- no pre-launch context
- no demo or proof
- no follow-up after announcement day

Good:
- problem education before launch
- clear reveal
- demo and customer proof
- follow-up content by use case
- conversion path stays visible

## Fail conditions

- audience does not understand why the launch matters
- assets repeat the same message without progression
- proof is missing
- announcement creates attention but no action
- follow-up plan is absent

## Enforcement rule

If a launch has only an announcement, build a sequence that teaches, proves, and converts.
