# Offer Architecture

## What
Offer Architecture structures what the user gets, why it matters, and what action they should take.

The offer is the packaged value, not just the product.

## Why it matters
Users act when the offer feels clear, valuable, credible, and easy to understand.

Strong offer architecture:
- clarifies what is included
- increases perceived value
- reduces uncertainty
- supports pricing and signup decisions
- makes CTAs more compelling

The offer should make the next step feel worthwhile.

## When to apply
- landing pages
- pricing pages
- email capture
- trials
- product launches
- upgrade flows
- checkout

## How to apply

- define the core outcome
- show what the user receives
- explain why it matters now
- include relevant proof or support
- reduce perceived risk
- make the CTA match the offer

The user should understand the exchange before acting.

## Implementation rules

- offer must be concrete
- included value must be visible
- bonus or supporting value must not distract from core value
- urgency must be honest
- terms must be clear
- CTA must complete the offer logically

## Example

Bad:
- "Sign up for updates"
- unclear deliverable
- no reason to act now
- vague benefits

Good:
- clear asset or outcome
- specific deliverables
- visible proof
- CTA tied to receiving the value

## Fail conditions

- user does not know what they get
- offer feels generic
- value is abstract
- CTA asks for more than the offer earns
- terms or expectations are unclear

## Enforcement rule

If the offer does not feel like a clear exchange of value, restructure it before asking for action.
