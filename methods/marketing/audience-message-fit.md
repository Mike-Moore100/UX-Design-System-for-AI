# Audience Message Fit

## What
Audience Message Fit means the message matches the specific needs, language, awareness, and urgency of the intended user.

The right message depends on who is reading.

## Why it matters
Generic messaging fails because different audiences care about different outcomes, risks, and proof.

Strong audience fit:
- increases relevance
- improves trust
- reduces explanation effort
- makes benefits more concrete
- improves conversion quality

The user should feel the product understands their situation.

## When to apply
- landing pages
- campaign pages
- homepages
- persona-specific pages
- onboarding
- sales pages
- email capture offers

## How to apply

- identify the user's role and context
- use language the audience already understands
- match benefits to their actual pain
- select proof relevant to that audience
- avoid explaining obvious things to experts
- avoid expert jargon for beginners

The copy should sound like it was written for the intended user.

## Implementation rules

- audience must be identifiable from the page
- benefits must map to audience-specific jobs
- proof must match audience trust criteria
- terminology must match audience sophistication
- objections must reflect real user concerns
- avoid one-size-fits-all messaging when audience is narrow

## Example

Bad:
- same page for founders, designers, developers, and enterprises
- broad claims with no audience context
- proof from irrelevant customer types

Good:
- message tailored to one audience
- benefits tied to their workflow
- relevant proof and examples
- language matching their expertise

## Fail conditions

- target user cannot tell it is for them
- benefits feel generic
- proof does not match the buyer or user
- tone is too simple or too technical
- objections are ignored

## Enforcement rule

If the audience is specific, the message must be specific to that audience.
