# Lead Magnet

## What
Lead Magnet design creates a valuable exchange where users willingly share contact details for a useful asset, tool, or outcome.

The value must be specific enough to justify the email ask.

## Why it matters
Users protect their inboxes.

A strong lead magnet:
- increases qualified leads
- builds trust before sales
- demonstrates expertise
- supports audience segmentation
- creates a useful reason to follow up

Weak lead magnets collect low-intent emails or fake addresses.

## When to apply
- newsletter capture
- resource downloads
- waitlists
- calculators
- templates
- webinars
- gated reports

## How to apply

- offer a concrete result, not vague content
- make the asset immediately useful
- show exactly what the user receives
- match the magnet to the user's current intent
- collect only the fields needed for delivery or segmentation
- set follow-up expectations clearly

The user should feel the exchange is fair.

## Implementation rules

- title must communicate the asset and outcome
- preview or contents must reduce uncertainty
- form must be shorter than the perceived value
- email consent and follow-up expectations must be clear
- delivery must be immediate or timing must be stated
- asset must connect to the product's broader value

## Example

Bad:
- "Get our newsletter"
- vague PDF with no preview
- many fields for a low-value asset
- unclear follow-up after signup

Good:
- specific checklist, template, calculator, or guide
- preview of contents
- simple email capture
- confirmation explains delivery and next steps

## Fail conditions

- user cannot tell what they receive
- asset does not match page intent
- form ask exceeds value
- follow-up is unclear
- magnet attracts leads unrelated to the product

## Enforcement rule

If the email exchange does not feel obviously valuable and honest, improve the lead magnet before optimising the form.
