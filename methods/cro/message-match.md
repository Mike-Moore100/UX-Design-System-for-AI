# Message Match

## What
Message Match keeps the promise a user clicked on aligned with the page, section, or step they land on.

The destination must confirm the user's intent immediately.

## Why it matters
Users lose trust when the click promise and destination feel disconnected.

Strong message match:
- reduces bounce
- increases relevance
- improves campaign quality
- lowers cognitive effort
- makes the next action feel natural

A mismatch makes the user re-evaluate whether they are in the right place.

## When to apply
- ad landing pages
- email campaign pages
- referral traffic
- product launches
- signup flows
- feature announcements

## How to apply

- mirror the source promise in the destination headline
- keep audience, offer, and outcome consistent
- match CTA language to the user's original intent
- preserve relevant proof from the source context
- remove unrelated claims that compete with the clicked promise
- adapt the page for high-intent versus exploratory traffic

The user should feel the page continues the conversation they already started.

## Implementation rules

- landing headline must reflect the clicked offer or intent
- supporting copy must not change the promise
- CTA must be the logical next step from the source
- proof must support the specific claim that drove the click
- page content must not introduce a different primary offer
- avoid generic homepages for specific campaign traffic

## Example

Bad:
- ad promises a pricing calculator, page opens on a generic product pitch
- email promotes a checklist, CTA asks for a demo first
- campaign headline and landing headline use different outcomes

Good:
- clicked promise repeated clearly
- relevant proof placed near the offer
- CTA continues the same intent
- page removes unrelated conversion paths

## Fail conditions

- user cannot tell why they landed there
- source promise and page promise differ
- CTA asks for a different commitment
- proof supports the brand but not the clicked claim
- traffic from different intents receives the same generic page

## Enforcement rule

If the destination does not immediately validate the user's click intent, fix message match before changing layout or CTA styling.
