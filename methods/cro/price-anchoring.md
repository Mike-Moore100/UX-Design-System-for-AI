# Price Anchoring

## What
Price Anchoring frames price against meaningful reference points so users can judge value more accurately.

Anchors must clarify value, not manipulate perception.

## Why it matters
Users evaluate price comparatively.

Good price anchoring:
- improves value comprehension
- reduces sticker shock
- supports plan comparison
- clarifies tradeoffs
- helps users choose confidently

Price without context feels expensive by default.

## When to apply
- pricing pages
- plan tables
- checkout screens
- upgrade prompts
- enterprise packages
- add-on pricing

## How to apply

- connect price to the outcome or cost avoided
- show meaningful plan differences
- make the recommended option rational, not merely highlighted
- compare against relevant alternatives when truthful
- use monthly, annual, seat, usage, or outcome framing carefully
- avoid fake discounts or inflated crossed-out prices

The user should understand why the price makes sense.

## Implementation rules

- anchors must be truthful and relevant
- plan hierarchy must make tradeoffs clear
- savings claims must be specific and verifiable
- recommended plans must explain why they are recommended
- price framing must not hide total cost
- avoid visual tricks that obscure lower-cost choices

## Example

Bad:
- fake original price
- recommended plan highlighted without reason
- annual price shown while monthly commitment is hidden
- vague "best value" label

Good:
- price connected to clear outcomes
- plan differences are concrete
- savings terms are transparent
- comparison supports user decision-making

## Fail conditions

- anchor is irrelevant to the user's decision
- discount framing is misleading
- total cost is difficult to understand
- plan choice is visually coerced
- value is implied but not explained

## Enforcement rule

If price is shown without a truthful value reference, add clear anchoring before optimising pricing visuals.
