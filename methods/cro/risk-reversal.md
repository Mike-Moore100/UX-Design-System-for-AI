# Risk Reversal

## What
Risk Reversal reduces the perceived downside of taking action.

It answers "What happens if this is not right for me?"

## Why it matters
Users often hesitate because of risk, not lack of interest.

Risk reversal:
- reduces fear of loss
- increases trial confidence
- supports purchase decisions
- makes commitment feel safer
- improves trust

The user should know the cost of being wrong is low.

## When to apply
- pricing pages
- trials
- checkout
- signup
- demos
- subscriptions
- high-commitment CTAs

## How to apply

- make cancellation terms clear
- state trial or refund terms near the CTA
- reduce commitment when possible
- explain what happens after clicking
- avoid hidden costs or surprise requirements
- make support and recovery visible

Risk reversal must be honest and easy to verify.

## Implementation rules

- no hidden fees or buried conditions
- trial terms must be close to trial CTAs
- refund or cancellation language must be plain
- "No credit card required" must be true if used
- next-step expectations must be clear
- risk reducers must not visually compete with the CTA

## Example

Bad:
- free trial requires surprise card entry
- cancellation terms hidden in footer
- "risk free" without explanation
- CTA does not explain next step

Good:
- clear trial terms near CTA
- cancellation explained plainly
- support or guarantee visible
- expectation-setting microcopy below the button

## Fail conditions

- user fears being trapped
- terms are vague or hidden
- CTA feels like a commitment trap
- risk reducers are not credible
- next step is unclear

## Enforcement rule

If hesitation comes from risk, make the downside clear, fair, and easy to escape.
