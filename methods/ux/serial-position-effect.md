# Serial Position Effect

## What
The Serial Position Effect means users are more likely to notice and remember items at the beginning and end of a sequence.

Order shapes attention.

## Why it matters
Lists, menus, plans, and steps compete for limited attention.

Good ordering:
- improves scanning
- supports decision-making
- makes priority visible
- reduces missed actions
- improves recall of key items

The most important items should not be buried in the middle.

## When to apply
- navigation
- pricing plans
- feature lists
- onboarding steps
- comparison tables
- menus
- checklists

## How to apply

- place the most important or common options first
- put final confirmation or next action at the end
- avoid burying critical actions in long lists
- group secondary items away from primary decisions
- use ordering to reflect priority and frequency
- keep long sequences scannable

The order should match what users need most.

## Implementation rules

- primary options should appear early or at the final decision point
- destructive actions should not be placed where accidental selection is likely
- long lists need grouping or filtering
- repeated sequences must keep consistent order
- final steps must clearly complete the flow
- do not order critical choices randomly or alphabetically by default if priority matters

## Example

Bad:
- primary plan hidden between less relevant plans
- key setting buried in the middle of a long list
- confirmation action placed away from the end of the flow

Good:
- most common plan positioned prominently
- high-frequency settings placed first
- final CTA appears after review
- long lists grouped by task priority

## Fail conditions

- important items are missed
- order does not reflect user priority
- long sequences feel random
- users must scan every item to find the likely choice
- final action is disconnected from the sequence

## Enforcement rule

If order affects user attention, place key items where they are easiest to notice and act on.
