# E-E-A-T Signals

## What
E-E-A-T Signals show experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness in content and page presentation.

The page should make it clear why users can rely on the information.

## Why it matters
Users and search systems both evaluate credibility.

Strong E-E-A-T signals:
- improves trust
- supports sensitive content quality
- clarifies accountability
- strengthens content usefulness
- reduces perceived risk

Unsupported claims feel weak, especially in high-stakes topics.

## When to apply
- advice content
- product comparisons
- health, finance, or legal-adjacent content
- technical guides
- reviews
- case studies
- security or privacy pages

## How to apply

- identify authors, contributors, or reviewers where relevant
- show first-hand experience through examples, screenshots, or process details
- support claims with evidence
- keep dates, disclosures, and corrections clear
- explain methodology for reviews or comparisons
- make contact, company, and trust information easy to find

The page should show who stands behind the content and why it is credible.

## Implementation rules

- author or organisation attribution must be clear when credibility matters
- factual claims must be supportable
- review and comparison criteria must be transparent
- dates must reflect creation or meaningful updates
- trust pages must be easy to access
- avoid anonymous claims on high-stakes topics

## Example

Bad:
- "best tools" list with no criteria
- anonymous expert advice
- outdated medical or financial claims
- no company or contact information

Good:
- author and review details
- transparent criteria
- real product screenshots or examples
- clear update date and sources where needed

## Fail conditions

- user cannot tell who created the content
- claims are unsupported
- methodology is hidden
- content is outdated
- trust details are hard to find

## Enforcement rule

If credibility affects the user's decision, add clear evidence, attribution, and accountability before optimising for keywords.
