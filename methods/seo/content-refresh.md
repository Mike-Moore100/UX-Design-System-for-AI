# Content Refresh

## What
Content Refresh keeps existing pages accurate, useful, and competitive as search intent, products, and evidence change.

Improving an existing page is often more valuable than publishing another weak page.

## Why it matters
Search performance decays when content becomes stale.

Good refresh work:
- improves accuracy
- protects rankings
- strengthens trust
- captures new intent
- reduces content bloat

Old content can silently become misleading.

## When to apply
- declining organic pages
- outdated guides
- product documentation
- comparison pages
- statistics pages
- pricing or feature content
- articles with changed search intent

## How to apply

- identify traffic, ranking, or conversion decline
- compare the page against current intent
- update facts, screenshots, examples, dates, and links
- remove outdated sections
- add missing questions or decision support
- consolidate overlapping pages when needed
- preserve URL equity where possible

The refreshed page should be more accurate and useful than the old version.

## Implementation rules

- factual claims must be current
- outdated screenshots or product details must be replaced
- page title and headings must match current intent
- internal links must be updated
- changed content should preserve valuable URLs unless migration is necessary
- do not update dates without meaningful content changes

## Example

Bad:
- changes publish date only
- leaves outdated screenshots
- adds generic paragraphs without answering new intent
- creates a duplicate article instead of improving the original

Good:
- updates examples and facts
- adds missing comparison criteria
- removes stale advice
- improves structure and internal links

## Fail conditions

- content remains outdated
- refresh adds length without usefulness
- URL equity is lost unnecessarily
- old claims conflict with product reality
- page no longer matches search intent

## Enforcement rule

If an existing page can better satisfy current intent, refresh or consolidate it before creating another similar page.
