# Topical Authority

## What
Topical Authority builds a coherent body of content that proves a site understands a subject deeply.

Authority comes from useful coverage, not isolated keyword pages.

## Why it matters
Search engines and users look for expertise across related questions.

Strong topical authority:
- improves relevance signals
- supports internal linking
- increases trust
- captures long-tail demand
- makes content strategy less random

Publishing disconnected articles weakens the site narrative.

## When to apply
- blogs
- documentation
- content hubs
- resource libraries
- category pages
- comparison pages
- educational landing pages

## How to apply

- define the core topic and user problem space
- map subtopics by user questions and decision stages
- create pillar and supporting content
- connect related pages with contextual internal links
- cover practical examples, edge cases, and comparisons
- update weak content rather than only adding new pages

The site should feel like a complete answer to a subject area.

## Implementation rules

- content clusters must map to real user intent
- pillar pages must connect to supporting pages
- supporting pages must link back where useful
- thin keyword-only pages must be avoided
- content must demonstrate practical expertise
- outdated or overlapping pages must be consolidated

## Example

Bad:
- dozens of shallow keyword posts
- no internal links between related topics
- pillar page is a generic marketing page
- content repeats without adding depth

Good:
- clear topic hub
- useful supporting guides
- comparison and implementation content
- internal links follow user questions

## Fail conditions

- content is disconnected
- pages compete for the same intent
- topic coverage is shallow
- internal links are generic
- content does not show expertise

## Enforcement rule

If content does not strengthen a clear topic cluster, refine the content plan before publishing more pages.
