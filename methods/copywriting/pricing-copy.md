# Pricing Copy

## What
Pricing Copy helps users understand plans, value, terms, and tradeoffs so they can choose confidently.

Pricing copy should reduce uncertainty at the highest-consideration point.

## Why it matters
Pricing pages concentrate risk, comparison, and objections.

Good pricing copy:
- clarifies value
- improves plan selection
- reduces billing anxiety
- answers objections
- supports qualified conversion

Unclear pricing creates mistrust.

## When to apply
- pricing pages
- plan cards
- checkout
- upgrade prompts
- trial start screens
- billing settings
- enterprise contact pages

## How to apply

- name plans by user fit or scale when useful
- explain who each plan is for
- make key differences visible
- clarify billing period, limits, and cancellation
- connect features to outcomes
- place reassurance near commitment actions

The user should know which plan fits and what happens after they choose.

## Implementation rules

- plan descriptions must explain fit
- price period and commitment must be clear
- included limits must be understandable
- CTA must describe the next step
- trial and billing terms must be visible
- enterprise copy must explain why contact is required

## Example

Bad:
- plan names only say Basic, Pro, Premium with no fit
- annual billing default is unclear
- "Contact us" with no reason
- feature lists use internal terms

Good:
- "For solo builders validating one product"
- "For teams shipping weekly"
- "No charge until trial ends"
- CTA says "Start 14-day trial"

## Fail conditions

- user cannot choose a plan
- price terms are unclear
- limits are hidden
- CTA creates uncertainty
- copy focuses on features without value

## Enforcement rule

If pricing copy does not make choice, value, and terms clear, fix the copy before changing plan layout.
