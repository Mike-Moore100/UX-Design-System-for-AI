# Voice And Tone

## What
Voice And Tone keeps copy recognisable while adapting the emotional register to the user's situation.

Voice is consistent; tone changes with context.

## Why it matters
Users experience copy as part of product behaviour.

Good voice and tone:
- builds trust
- improves comprehension
- reduces emotional friction
- makes the product feel coherent
- protects sensitive moments

Wrong tone can make even correct information feel careless.

## When to apply
- onboarding
- errors
- success states
- billing
- security prompts
- marketing pages
- product notifications

## How to apply

- define the brand voice in usable writing rules
- adapt tone to user emotion and risk
- use calm direct language in high-stakes moments
- keep celebratory language proportional
- remove jokes where the user may be frustrated
- keep terminology consistent across surfaces

The copy should sound like the same product responding appropriately to the moment.

## Implementation rules

- tone must match user context
- serious states must use direct, calm copy
- playful copy must not obscure meaning
- terminology must be consistent
- voice rules must include examples
- clarity must override personality when they conflict

## Example

Bad:
- "Oopsie!" after payment failure
- formal legal tone during simple onboarding
- different names for the same feature
- joke in a data-loss warning

Good:
- calm error explanation
- concise success message
- supportive onboarding prompt
- consistent product terms

## Fail conditions

- tone feels inappropriate
- voice changes by screen without reason
- copy is memorable but unclear
- serious moments feel dismissive
- product terminology drifts

## Enforcement rule

If tone does not match the user's emotional state, rewrite it before polishing style.
