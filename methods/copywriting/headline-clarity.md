# Headline Clarity

## What
Headline Clarity makes the first line of a page, section, or screen communicate the main idea quickly.

A headline should orient the user before it tries to impress them.

## Why it matters
Users decide whether to keep reading from the first meaningful line.

Clear headlines:
- improve comprehension
- increase relevance
- reduce bounce
- support scanning
- make CTAs easier to evaluate

If the headline is vague, the rest of the page works harder.

## When to apply
- homepages
- landing pages
- feature pages
- product screens
- onboarding steps
- empty states
- pricing sections

## How to apply

- name the product, outcome, or user job plainly
- put the strongest meaning first
- avoid abstract hype
- make section headlines explain the section's role
- pair emotional hooks with concrete support
- test whether the headline makes sense without surrounding copy

The user should know what the page or section is about immediately.

## Implementation rules

- headline must communicate the primary idea
- supporting copy must expand, not rescue, the headline
- avoid slogans that hide meaning
- section headings must support scanning
- headline and CTA must not conflict
- do not use cleverness at the cost of comprehension

## Example

Bad:
- "Work without limits"
- "The future starts here"
- "Level up your team"
- section headline says "Powerful features"

Good:
- "Ship AI-built interfaces with production UX rules"
- "Create a secure workspace for every client"
- "Compare plans by team size and support level"

## Fail conditions

- user cannot tell what is being offered
- headline could describe many products
- supporting copy must explain the basic meaning
- section headings are decorative
- headline creates expectations the page does not meet

## Enforcement rule

If a headline does not communicate the main idea on its own, rewrite it before editing surrounding copy.
