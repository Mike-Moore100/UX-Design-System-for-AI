# Objection Copy

## What
Objection Copy directly answers the concerns that prevent users from moving forward.

It should reduce uncertainty with useful information, not generic reassurance.

## Why it matters
Users hesitate when risk, effort, cost, or trust is unclear.

Good objection copy:
- improves confidence
- reduces abandonment
- supports conversion
- lowers support questions
- makes commitments feel safer

The strongest copy often answers the question users are afraid to ask.

## When to apply
- pricing
- checkout
- signup
- demo requests
- cancellation
- security pages
- data collection
- upgrade prompts

## How to apply

- identify likely objections by decision point
- answer the objection near the relevant CTA or field
- be specific about terms, timing, cost, and control
- use proof where reassurance is not enough
- avoid dismissing valid concerns
- keep answers concise but complete

The user should feel informed enough to continue.

## Implementation rules

- objection copy must answer a real concern
- risk-related claims must be truthful
- terms must be clear before commitment
- sensitive data asks must explain purpose
- proof must support reassurance
- avoid vague phrases like "don't worry" without detail

## Example

Bad:
- "No risk" with no explanation
- asks for card details without billing timing
- "Secure and trusted" without detail
- hides cancellation policy

Good:
- "No charge until your trial ends on 12 June"
- "Cancel from Settings at any time"
- "We use this to create your workspace URL"
- security details linked near enterprise CTA

## Fail conditions

- major concern is unanswered
- copy reassures without evidence
- terms are hidden
- answer appears after abandonment point
- copy minimises a legitimate risk

## Enforcement rule

If hesitation is predictable, answer it directly at the point where it affects action.
